Varmala preservation has grown popular fast, and like anything that grows fast online, it has accumulated a fog of half-truths — glossy reels promising flowers "exactly as fresh forever", and equally confident warnings that all resin "turns yellow in a year". Neither is true, and couples deserve better than either extreme. A preserved varmala is a genuinely wonderful thing, but it is wonderful in specific, honest ways. This post walks through the most common myths one by one, so you can commission a piece with real expectations — and love the result for what it actually is.

## Myth 1: "The flowers will look exactly like they did at the wedding"

**The truth: colours deepen and shift — beautifully, but noticeably.**

Drying changes flowers. It has to; removing the water that makes up most of a petal inevitably changes how that petal holds light. The shifts are consistent enough to predict:

- **Red roses** deepen into rich maroon and burgundy tones — arguably more dramatic than fresh red.
- **White and cream flowers** mellow toward ivory, champagne and soft beige. Bright white does not survive drying anywhere.
- **Pinks** hold reasonably well, usually settling a shade or two deeper.
- **Yellows and oranges** (marigold territory) mute somewhat but keep their warmth.
- **Greens** in foliage darken toward olive and sage.

A skilled studio works *with* these shifts, composing layouts that celebrate the deepened palette rather than pretending it away. If someone promises your flowers will stay ceremony-fresh in colour, they are selling you the reel, not the craft. The preserved piece looks like your varmala remembered — warmer, deeper, more antique — not your varmala photographed.

> A preserved varmala is not a photograph of the flowers; it is the flowers themselves, grown wiser in colour.

## Myth 2: "All resin turns yellow within a year"

**The truth: quality resin, cured properly and placed sensibly, stays clear for many years.**

This myth persists because it used to be closer to true. Cheap or outdated resins yellowed readily, and plenty of low-cost pieces still do. But modern art-grade epoxy formulated with UV stabilisers behaves very differently: kept out of prolonged direct sunlight, it remains water-clear for years and ages gracefully thereafter. Three factors decide the outcome:

1. **Resin grade** — art-grade epoxy with UV inhibitors versus bargain casting resin. This is a large part of what separates studio pricing from marketplace pricing.
2. **Correct curing** — properly measured, mixed and layer-cured resin is chemically stable; rushed work is not.
3. **Placement** — a frame on a wall that gets direct afternoon sun through a window will amber faster than one on a shaded wall, whatever the resin. Hang it out of harsh light and you have removed the biggest variable yourself.

Very gradual warming over a decade or more is honest physics. Dramatic yellowing within a year or two signals poor materials or poor process — not an inherent flaw of the medium.

## Myth 3: "Preservation is just dipping the garland in resin"

**The truth: the resin is the last 20 per cent; drying is the craft.**

If a fresh garland were simply submerged in resin, the trapped moisture would cloud the casting and rot the petals from the inside within months. Real preservation means unstringing the garland, sorting the blooms, and drying every flower for weeks in silica gel before any resin is mixed — then casting in thin layers over several days so heat and bubbles never get the upper hand. This is also why genuine preservation takes six to ten weeks and cannot honestly be delivered in one. ## Myth 4: "LED lighting will damage or fade the flowers"

**The truth: quality LEDs run cool and sit outside the casting — the flowers never feel them.**

The instinct is understandable: light fades flowers, so surely a lit frame is a slow-motion mistake? But the fading culprit in sunlight is ultraviolet radiation and heat, and decent LED strips emit negligible amounts of either. In a well-made lit frame, the LEDs are mounted in the frame or base, throwing light *through* the resin rather than baking against petals. The sealed casting does the rest — the flowers are isolated from air and moisture regardless of whether the light is on. Choose warm white, place the frame away from direct sun, and the lighting is a pleasure, not a risk.

## Myth 5: "Once it's in resin, it needs zero care forever"

**The truth: it needs almost no care — but "almost" is doing some work in that sentence.**

Resin is dramatically more durable than ceramic or glass and asks very little. But three small habits protect the piece across decades: keep it off walls with prolonged direct sunlight, dust it with a soft, slightly damp microfibre cloth rather than a dry rub, and keep solvents — acetone, thinners, harsh sprays — away from the surface. That is genuinely the whole regimen. Skip these and the piece will not fail; follow them and it will still look freshly polished when your children ask about it.

## Myth 6: "Any garland can be preserved perfectly, no matter its condition"

**The truth: preservation captures your flowers as they arrive — it cannot rewind them.**

This is the myth that causes the most heartache, so it deserves the plainest telling. Drying and casting arrest decay; they do not reverse it. A garland refrigerated the night of the wedding and handed over within days preserves gloriously. One that spent a week in a sealed plastic bag arrives with browning and possible mould, and while much may still be salvageable, the result will honestly reflect that history. An ethical studio inspects the flowers at intake and tells you exactly what to expect before you commit — and if some blooms are beyond saving, a thoughtful layout built from the best of them often matters more than completeness. The practical defence is simple and free: know the storage steps before the wedding, and assign someone to follow them.
