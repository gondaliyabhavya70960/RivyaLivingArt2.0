Trends in home decor arrive loudly and leave quietly. Terrazzo had its year. Rose gold had its three. Yet if you walk through any resin studio today — including ours — the colour that clients ask for most, decade after decade, is blue. Ocean pours with white-lace waves. Geode rings in navy and teal wrapped in gold. Midnight tabletops that look like deep water at dusk. Blue resin is not a trend that peaked; it is a category that settled in and stayed. Understanding why tells you a great deal about what makes any handcrafted piece worth commissioning.

## Blue is the colour we never tire of looking at

Colour preference studies across cultures come back to the same finding again and again: blue is the world's most consistently loved colour. Psychologists connect it to the two things humans have stared at longest — sky and water. Both are vast, both move slowly, and both signal calm and safety. When you cast blue into resin, you get something neither paint nor fabric can offer: depth. Light travels into a blue resin pour, bounces between pigment layers, and comes back changed. The surface looks less like a coloured object and more like a window into water.

That depth is the real reason ocean pieces endure. A printed wave on canvas is a picture of the sea. A resin wave — built in translucent layers, with cells of white blown across the crest — behaves like the sea. It shifts as you walk past it. It reads differently at noon and at lamplight. Owners tell us the same thing in different words: the piece never becomes wallpaper.

## The ocean style: movement frozen mid-motion

Ocean resin work is built from layered pours — typically a deep base of navy or indigo, a mid-band of teal or turquoise, a shallow band of seafoam, and finally the white "lacing" that mimics foam. The lacing is the signature of the style and the hardest part to do well. It cannot be painted on; it is created by manipulating a lighter, heat-reactive white resin so that it blooms into organic cells across the surface. No two pours lace the same way, which is precisely the appeal. Your tray or tabletop carries a wave that exists nowhere else.

Ocean styling suits pieces you interact with at close range, where the layering rewards a second look:

- **Serving pieces** — an ocean-edge board or platter turns an ordinary chai tray into a small event; see our [resin trays and serving platters](/shop/resin-trays-serving-platters) for the shapes this works best on.
- **Wall art and clocks** — a wave frozen across a clock face gives a functional object genuine presence.
- **Tabletops and consoles** — the largest canvas for lacing, and the most dramatic.

## The geode style: geology, interpreted

Where ocean work is about movement, geode work is about structure. A resin geode imitates the cross-section of a split agate or amethyst: a crystalline "druzy" centre (often crushed glass or fire glass), concentric rings of translucent and opaque colour, and metallic veining — usually gold leaf — separating the bands. Blue dominates here too, but in a different register: sapphire, lapis, petrol, ink. Paired with gold, blue geodes read unmistakably as luxury, which is why they have become favourites for pooja room accents, entryway art and statement [art and craft pieces](/shop/art-craft-pieces).

Geode work rewards patience in the making. Each ring is a separate pour, and the metallic veining is laid by hand between cures. A well-made geode panel can involve a week of staged work before it ever reaches the polishing wheel.

> Blue endures because it is not really a colour choice. It is a choice of depth — and depth never goes out of style.

## Choosing your blue: a practical shade guide

Not all blues live happily in all rooms. A few honest pairings from studio experience:

- **Navy and indigo** — the safest long-term choice. Reads as classic against wood tones, brass and warm whites. Ideal for furniture and large wall pieces you intend to keep for decades.
- **Teal and peacock** — the most "Indian" of the blues; it sits beautifully with jewel-toned textiles, mustard, and traditional brass. Wonderful in trays and smaller decor.
- **Turquoise and seafoam** — coastal and airy, best in bathrooms, kids' spaces and light-filled rooms. Be careful pairing it with grey-heavy interiors, where it can feel cold.
- **Ink and midnight (near-black blues)** — dramatic and modern; superb with gold veining, best used in one statement piece rather than repeated across a room.

If your room already carries strong colour, choose a blue two steps deeper than anything in it — the resin should anchor, not compete.

## Why this palette ages gracefully

The practical case for blue is as strong as the emotional one. Quality blue pigments — particularly the mineral-derived and mica-based ones — are among the most lightfast colours available to resin artists, meaning they resist fading far better than many reds and violets. Blue also forgives the natural, very slow warming that all epoxy undergoes over many years: a whisper of amber over navy simply deepens it, where the same shift over a pale pink would be visible. A blue piece commissioned today is a piece that will still look intentional in twenty years.

That longevity matters more in made-to-order work than anywhere else. When something is built for you — sized to your console, coloured to your wall — you want the palette itself to have staying power. Blue has proven it does.
