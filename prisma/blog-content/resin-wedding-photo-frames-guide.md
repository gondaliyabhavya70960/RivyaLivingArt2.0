A wedding photograph in an ordinary frame is a picture on a wall. The same photograph sealed inside hand-poured resin becomes something closer to an object — the image sits beneath a glass-clear layer with real depth, often surrounded by preserved florals, gold leaf or lettering, and the whole piece catches light the way a polished stone does. If you are considering a resin frame for your wedding photo, this guide walks through the choices that actually matter: the base and border, whether LED backlighting suits your photo, and — the part most people overlook until it is too late — the quality of the photograph itself.

## What makes a resin photo frame different

In a conventional frame, the photo sits behind glass and can be swapped out next year. In a resin frame, the printed photograph is embedded — sealed permanently within layers of clear resin, alongside whatever else the design calls for: dried flowers from the wedding itself, flakes of gold or silver leaf, a strip of the invitation card, names and the wedding date in hand-placed lettering.

That permanence changes how you should think about the piece. You are not buying a frame; you are commissioning a single fixed artwork built around one photograph. Which is exactly why the decisions below deserve ten minutes of proper thought.

> A resin frame is not a holder for a photo — it is the photo, the flowers and the date fused into one object you cannot take apart and would never want to.

## Teakwood borders: warmth and weight

The most requested pairing in Indian homes is clear resin with a teakwood border or base, and there are good reasons for it beyond fashion.

- **Visual warmth.** Resin alone reads modern and glassy. A teak border grounds it, so the piece sits comfortably next to traditional furniture, brass décor and warm-toned walls.
- **Structural honesty.** Wood gives the frame weight and rigidity, which matters for larger wall pieces. A well-seasoned hardwood border resists the slight flexing that can stress a large resin panel over years.
- **Ageing gracefully.** Teak deepens in colour over time. A frame that looks handsome at the wedding looks even better at the tenth anniversary.

If your home leans contemporary — white walls, minimal lines — a borderless resin block with polished edges may suit you better. There is no wrong answer, only a wrong match with your room. Browse the range of [wedding photo frames](/shop/wedding-photo-frames) to see both directions side by side.

## LED backlighting: when it works, and when it doesn't

LED frames have a warm strip of lighting set behind or around the resin panel, so the piece glows in the evening. It is a genuinely lovely effect — but it is not right for every photograph or every room.

### Where LED shines

- **Bedrooms and low-light corners.** The soft glow doubles as a night lamp, and couples often say it becomes the last thing they look at before sleep.
- **Frames with embedded florals.** Backlighting makes preserved petals luminous, revealing texture you cannot see in daylight.
- **Darker photographs.** An evening pheras shot or a dim mandap photo gains life when lit from within.

### Where it doesn't

- **Bright, sun-filled walls.** In a room flooded with daylight, the LED effect is invisible for most of the day; you are paying for a feature you rarely see.
- **Very light, airy photographs.** A bright daytime portrait can look washed out when backlit.
- **Walls with no socket nearby.** Practical, but real — plan the placement before you choose the feature. Battery options exist but need recharging discipline.

A sensible rule: choose LED for where the frame will live, not for how it looks in someone else's reel.

## The photo itself: quality decides everything

This is the section to read twice. The resin, the wood, the craftsmanship — all of it frames one photograph, and no artisan can fix a low-resolution image. Before you commission anything, check the following.

- **Send the original file, not a screenshot.** Ask your photographer for the full-resolution export. WhatsApp compresses images heavily, so share the file as a document, through a drive link, or by email — never as a regular photo message.
- **Resolution matters more than megapixels sound.** As a working guide, a photo printed at 8x10 inches wants roughly 2400x3000 pixels or better. Professional wedding photos exceed this easily; phone screenshots do not.
- **Choose a photo with a clear subject.** Wide shots with forty guests shrink faces to dots. The frames that move people are almost always two faces, close, in a genuine moment — the varmala exchange, the sindoor, a laugh between rituals.
- **Mind the crop.** Frames are often square or near-square; your photo may be 3:2 or 16:9. Decide what can be cropped away before the design stage, or choose a photo with generous space around the couple.
- **Colour and contrast survive resin well — softness does not.** Slightly blurred images become obviously blurred under a glossy layer that invites close viewing.

If you are unsure whether a particular file will hold up, send it across before ordering — an honest yes-or-no on print quality takes minutes and saves heartbreak.

## Pairing the frame with preserved florals

The most meaningful resin frames combine the photograph with material from the day itself: petals from the varmala, a jasmine string from the bride's hair, a fragment of the invitation. If your wedding flowers are still with you — even dried — they can often be incorporated, and the frame becomes a companion piece to a full [varmala preservation](/shop/varmala-preservation) artwork, or a beautiful alternative if the complete garland was not kept.

Flowers intended for embedding should be kept dry, pressed between paper or air-dried in a ventilated spot, and away from plastic bags where trapped moisture causes browning. Even a small handful of petals changes the character of the finished frame entirely.

## A short checklist before you order

- Full-resolution photo file ready (not a screenshot or WhatsApp-compressed image)
- Placement decided — wall or tabletop, and whether a socket is nearby for LED
- Border preference: teakwood warmth or borderless modern
- Any florals, invitation pieces or fabric you want embedded, kept dry and ready
- Names, date and any short line of text finalised — spelling checked twice, because resin does not forgive typos
