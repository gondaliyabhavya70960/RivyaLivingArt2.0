There is a quiet genius in the old tradition of anniversary gifts by year. Paper for the first year, cotton for the second, wood for the fifth — each material grows sturdier as the marriage does. The list was never really about the materials. It was a way of saying: this bond began as something delicate, and every year it becomes harder to break. Resin, as it happens, is the perfect medium for honouring that tradition, because it can hold almost any of those materials inside it — paper, fabric, flowers, wood — and seal them into something that lasts for decades.

Here is how to read the traditional list through an artisan's eyes, year by year.

## Years One to Five: Preserving the Delicate Things

The early years are assigned fragile materials for a reason, and they happen to be the materials resin preserves most beautifully.

**Year one — paper.** The obvious move is a card or a book. The memorable move is preserving actual paper from the wedding itself: the invitation card, a handwritten vow, a boarding pass from the honeymoon. Cast flat within clear resin, a wedding invitation becomes a piece of art you can stand on a shelf — the gold foil and lettering suspended as if the day were yesterday.

**Year two — cotton.** A swatch from the wedding outfit — a sliver of the saree border, a piece of the sherwani lining — can be embedded in a small tray or paperweight. It sounds unusual until you see it done well; fabric under resin takes on a jewel-like depth.

**Year three — leather.** Resin does not preserve leather well, so here the tradition is better honoured in spirit: pair a classic leather gift with a small resin accent, like a personalised keychain carrying initials and the wedding date.

**Year four — fruit and flowers.** This is the year resin was made for. If any flowers from the wedding were kept — pressed petals, a dried rose from the bouquet — they can be arranged and preserved permanently. Couples who saved their varmala have an even richer option, which we will come to.

**Year five — wood.** Think river-style pieces where timber and tinted resin meet in a flowing seam: a cheese board, a small side table, a serving platter. Wood-and-resin work from our [furniture and surfaces collection](/shop/resin-furniture-surfaces) suits this year perfectly — solid, warm, and built for daily use, exactly what year five should feel like.

## Years Six to Ten: Gifts That Enter Daily Life

The middle years assign homely materials — sugar, copper, pottery, tin. The theme is domesticity: by now the marriage lives in shared routines, so the gifts should too.

- **Year six (sugar/sweets):** a set of resin coasters or a small serving tray for the evening chai ritual — sweetness, served daily.
- **Year seven (copper/wool):** resin takes metallic pigments gorgeously; a copper-veined tray or bowl nods to the tradition without a hint of cliché.
- **Year eight (pottery) and nine (willow):** handmade vessels and woven textures translate into sculptural resin vases or a catch-all bowl for the dresser.
- **Year ten (tin/aluminium):** the first big milestone. Ten years deserves a statement — a wall clock with pigment work in the couple's wedding colours, or a framed piece marking the decade.

> The early years give you fragile materials on purpose — and resin exists to make fragile things permanent.

## The Milestone Years: Fifteen, Twenty, Twenty-Five

**Year fifteen — crystal.** Clear, glass-like resin is the honest artisan's answer to crystal. A sculptural centrepiece or a pair of elegant trays with a water-clear finish captures the brief: transparency, light, permanence.

**Year twenty — china.** Rather than another dinner set destined for the display cabinet, consider a curated tablescape — chargers, coasters, napkin ring accents — designed as one family of colours. It is china's spirit (a beautiful shared table) without china's fate (a locked cupboard).

**Year twenty-five — silver.** The silver jubilee calls for the most sentimental piece in this entire guide. If the couple's varmala — or even a few flowers from it — was saved and dried, it can be preserved in a resin block, frame, or table piece. [Varmala preservation](/shop/varmala-preservation) turns the very garlands exchanged at the wedding into a permanent object, often finished with silver leaf, the wedding date, or a portrait alongside. If the flowers are long gone, a preserved-flower frame using the same varieties, paired with a wedding photograph, carries the same weight.

## Making It Personal: The Details That Matter

Whatever the year, the difference between a nice gift and an heirloom is specificity. Before commissioning, gather two or three of these:

- The exact wedding date, and the couple's names as they like them written
- The wedding colour palette (a photo of the decor or outfits is enough)
- Any physical fragments: invitation card, pressed flowers, fabric swatch, a hotel key card from the honeymoon
- A line from their wedding vows, a song lyric, or a phrase they use with each other

An artisan can build a piece around any one of these. Two or three together, and the gift stops being an object and becomes a retelling of their story.

## A Practical Note on Timelines and Budgets

Made-to-order resin work cannot be rushed — curing alone takes days, and layered pieces with embedded elements take longer. As a rule of thumb, allow two to four weeks for most commissions, and more for large or intricate milestone pieces. If the anniversary is close, ask; sometimes a simpler design can be completed sooner, and it is always better to gift a thoughtful piece a week late than a rushed one on time.

On budget: small personalised keepsakes generally begin in the high hundreds to low thousands of rupees, everyday pieces like trays and frames sit in the low-to-mid thousands, and large preservation work or furniture-scale pieces range well beyond that depending on size and complexity. Share your budget honestly when you enquire through a [custom order](/custom-order) — a good artisan will design to it rather than upsell past it.

The anniversary list has survived for over a century because it understands something true: love deserves to be marked in materials, not just words. Choose the material for the year, add the details only the two of them would recognise, and you have a gift that will still be on their shelf at the next milestone — and the one after that.
